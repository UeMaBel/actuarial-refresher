# Correlation and Dependence

For random variables $X$ and $Y$, covariance is $\operatorname{Cov}(X,Y)=\mathbb{E}[(X-\mathbb{E}X)(Y-\mathbb{E}Y)]$. Correlation scales covariance to lie between $-1$ and $1$ when both variances are positive.

Correlation captures linear association, not the whole joint distribution. Zero correlation does not generally imply independence, and variables with modest ordinary correlation can still have substantial joint tail risk.

## Actuarial importance

Dependence affects aggregate claims, diversification, reinsurance, and capital. Motor claims may accumulate after a common event; health risks may share medical-cost inflation; mortality across a portfolio can be affected by a catastrophe. Financial scenario generators also need coherent dependence across rates, equity, inflation, and credit variables.

Dependence assumptions should reflect the purpose and stress conditions of the model. Historical correlations alone may understate joint extremes.
