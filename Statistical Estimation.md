# Statistical Estimation

Statistical estimation uses data to infer unknown model parameters. An estimate is not the true parameter with certainty; its uncertainty should be considered in pricing, reserving, and risk analysis.

## Common approaches

- **Maximum likelihood:** Choose parameters that make the observed data most likely under the model.
- **Method of moments:** Match sample moments, such as the mean and variance, to their model counterparts.
- **Bayesian estimation:** Combine a prior distribution with observed data to obtain a posterior distribution.
- **Credibility:** Blend experience with broader or prior expectations, with the weight depending on the amount and relevance of data.

## Uncertainty and diagnostics

Standard errors, confidence intervals, likelihood profiles, or posterior intervals describe parameter uncertainty under stated assumptions. Check data quality, exposure definitions, outliers, model fit, and stability over time. A narrow interval under a misspecified model can give false confidence.

In insurance, rare events and long-tailed claims often provide limited data. External information, expert judgement, and sensitivity tests can therefore be important alongside statistical estimates.
