# Solvency II - Pillar II - Governance and Risk Management

> Ensure that the insurer understands, controls, and plans for its risks.


## System of governance

The insurer needs clear responsibilities, effective controls, and appropriate decision-making processes.

Four key functions are:

- **Risk management:** Identify, measure, monitor, manage, and report risks.
- **Compliance:** Assess compliance with applicable requirements.
- **Internal audit:** Independently assess governance and internal controls.
- **Actuarial function:** Coordinate technical-provision work, assess methods and data, and contribute to risk management.

Outsourcing work does not remove the insurer’s responsibility for it.

## ORSA — Own Risk and Solvency Assessment

ORSA connects the insurer’s risk profile, strategy, business plan, and capital position.

It considers:

- Overall solvency needs
- Continued compliance with capital and technical-provision requirements
- Whether the risk profile differs materially from assumptions underlying the SCR calculation

ORSA is a decision-making process, not just a report or a repetition of the SCR calculation.

## Planning and scenario analysis

Real-World Scenarios support projections of:

- Profits
- Capital resources and requirements
- Liquidity
- Investment performance
- Future business development

An Economic Scenario Generator can supply financial paths for these projections.

Stress tests examine adverse conditions. Reverse stress tests ask which conditions could cause the business model to fail.

## Asset–liability management

The insurer examines how assets and obligations interact through:

- Interest rates
- Inflation
- Reinvestment
- Cash-flow timing
- Policyholder behaviour
- Management actions

Applications differ across:

- Life Insurance
- Motor Insurance
- Health Insurance

## Model governance

Models and scenario sets need:

- A defined purpose
- Defensible calibration
- Reliable data
- Validation
- Documented limitations
- Change controls
- Appropriate use by decision-makers

More simulation paths reduce numerical noise; they do not correct poor assumptions.

Vendor validation or certification can support assurance, but does not replace the insurer’s own oversight.
