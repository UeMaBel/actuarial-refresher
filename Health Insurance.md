# Health Insurance

> Health insurance covers medical costs or provides benefits linked to illness, treatment, or incapacity.

Its structure varies considerably across countries and products.

German statutory health insurance — GKV — is a social-insurance system. German private health insurance — PKV — is a different system, often involving long-duration contracts and funded ageing provisions.

## Main products

| Product | Main protection |
|---|---|
| Medical expense insurance | Reimbursement or payment of eligible treatment costs |
| Supplementary health insurance | Additional benefits beyond primary coverage |
| Daily sickness allowance — Krankentagegeld | Income-related daily benefit during qualifying incapacity |
| Hospital cash — Krankenhaustagegeld | Fixed daily benefit during qualifying hospital stays |

Long-term care insurance is related but covers a distinct need and can have different rules.

## Main actuarial risks

- **Morbidity:** Incidence and severity of illness differ from expectations.
- **Utilisation:** Insured people use more treatment or services.
- **Medical inflation:** Treatment prices and costs increase.
- **Medical technology:** New treatments change utilisation and cost.
- **Longevity and ageing:** Coverage continues into ages with higher expected costs.
- **Lapse and selection:** Departing and remaining policyholders have different risk profiles.
- **Expense:** Administration costs exceed assumptions.
- **Interest-rate risk:** Investment income or discount rates change.

Medical inflation can differ substantially from general consumer-price inflation.

## Two broad modelling structures

### Life-like health business

Long-duration contracts may require projections of:

- Mortality
- Morbidity
- Lapses
- Expenses
- Investment returns
- Premium adjustments
- Future benefits over many years

### Non-life-like health business

Shorter-duration contracts may focus more on:

- Claim frequency
- Claim severity
- Annual pricing
- Claims development
- Outstanding claims reserves

Under Solvency II, this distinction is reflected in health business pursued on a similar technical basis to life insurance — SLT — versus non-life insurance — NSLT.

## German private health insurance: ageing provisions

For relevant long-term PKV products, expected medical costs generally increase with age.

Part of the premium in earlier years helps build **ageing provisions — Alterungsrückstellungen** to finance higher expected costs later.

These are actuarial provisions within an insurance system, not simply an unrestricted personal savings account.

Actual financing also depends on investment income, claims experience, contractual provisions, and applicable premium-adjustment rules.

## Role of economic scenarios

An Economic Scenario Generator can support projections of:

- Investment income
- Interest rates
- Discount factors
- General inflation
- Asset–liability interactions

Separate health-cost models are needed for medical inflation, utilisation, morbidity, and treatment trends.

Real-World Scenarios support planning and risk analysis. Risk-Neutral Scenarios may support financial-option valuation where relevant.

## Solvency II connection

Solvency II distinguishes:

- Health underwriting risk on a life-like basis — SLT
- Health underwriting risk on a non-life-like basis — NSLT
- Health catastrophe risk

The applicable treatment depends on the technical characteristics of the obligations, not only the product label.

## IFRS 17 connection

Under IFRS, long-duration health contracts may require the General Measurement Model.

Eligible shorter-duration contracts may use the Premium Allocation Approach.

The measurement approach depends on contract characteristics and eligibility criteria.
