# Statistics

Statistics uses observed data to describe variation, estimate unknown quantities, and assess uncertainty. In actuarial work, data may include policy exposures, claim counts and amounts, mortality experience, expenses, or market returns.

## Descriptive statistics

The sample mean summarizes the average; quantiles describe locations in the distribution; variance and standard deviation describe spread. For insurance data, always identify the exposure basis, observation period, censoring, and any changes in definitions or portfolios.

## Inference and dependence

A statistical model links observations to unknown parameters. Estimates are uncertain, especially with sparse data or rare events. Correlation measures linear association, but does not fully describe dependence or tail co-movement. Independence is a stronger assumption and should be justified before using it in aggregate risk calculations.

## Actuarial use

Estimate claim frequency and severity separately, project claims development, and compare observed mortality or morbidity with assumptions. Statistical fit is only one part of model choice: credibility, judgement, data quality, stability, and the intended use matter too. Sensitivity and scenario analysis show how parameter uncertainty affects results.
